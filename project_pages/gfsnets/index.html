Generating Fast and Slow: Curricula in Structured Generative Models for Scene Decomposition

Mihir Prabhudesai Anirudh Goyal Deepak Pathak Katerina Fragkiadaki
Carnegie Mellon University | Mila, University of Montreal


Abstract

Scenes are composed of objects and objects are composed of parts, but this entity-centric compositionality has not been fully exploited thus far in the predominantly multilayer networks. Most existing generative models map the whole input scene into a latent vector without decomposing the scene into objects and objects into parts at any point during encoding or decoding. Visual detectors of objects and parts, though impressive within their training distributions, often fail to segment a novel scene into its constituent entities. In this paper, we revisit the problem of scene or object decomposition using slot-centric autoencoder models and highlight two important ingredients for success in nontoy environments: i) curriculum training from simple (primitive) to complex structures and ii) test-time adaptation on a single scene via gradient descent on the reconstruction objective. We demonstrate an initial primitive training helps break the symmetry problem currently handicapping slotcentric generative models, in which points in the scene equally prefer multiple entity-slots during iterative routing and thus fail to decompose the scene into disjoint entities. We further demonstrate that finetuning via SGD on the reconstruction objective (when pretrained over primitives) effectively segments a truly novel scene into parts by projecting unfamiliar input onto a familiar distribution of primitives. We compare GFS-Nets for segmentation against 3D-DETR (SOTA detector) trained supervised on the same training set, against unsupervised generative parsing methods such as Slot Attention, and against various other unsupervised and supervised segmentation baselines in various benchmarks, such as ShapeNet, PartNet, CLEVR, Room Diverse++, and show large (∼ 50%) performance improvements.


Overview of GFS-Nets


Point-cloud and image decompositions with Generative Fast and Slow Networks (GFS-Nets). GFS-Nets parses completely novel scenes into familiar entities via slow inference, i.e., gradient descent on the reconstruction error of the scene under consideration. Left: We train supervised both our model and 3D-DETR, a SOTA pointcloud 3D part detector, to segment 3D parts in pointclouds of the PartNet Chair category and test them on unseen categories of PartNet. Our model outperforms 3D-DETR by 50% in segmentation accuracy using self-supervised slow inference in the test point-clouds. Right: We test GFS-Nets in unsupervised segmentation given a curriculum in training with single object scenes. Our model significantly outperforms the baseline generative models of Slot Attention and uORF.


Stages of training in GFS-Nets.. Left: Learning the primitive distribution. First, GFS-Nets is trained to auto encode each primitive using few slots (one in the case of 3D part primitives, two in the case of single object images, one for the foreground, and one for the background. Right: Learning to de-compose complex scenes. Next, GFS-Nets is applied on complex scenes that are comprised on multiple primitives (parts or objects) in complex configurations. We instantiate multiple slots, their number is an upper bound of the number of primitives in the dataset. GFS-Nets then autoencodes the scene while binding regions of the input to different slots, and slots that are unused are mapped to the empty set. Aside of jointly autoencoding the whole dataset of complex scenes, GFS-Nets further uses gradient descent on the reconstruction objective in each indivisual scene—we call it slow inference—to improve the decomposition accuracy
